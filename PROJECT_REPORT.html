1. Introduction & Objectives

Phishing represents the most prevalent attack vector in network security and social engineering. Attackers routinely exploit human psychology (urgency, authority, greed, and fear) while bypassing traditional network perimeter defenses through domain spoofing, typosquatting, raw IP links, and text-only Business Email Compromise (BEC).

The objective of this Project-Based Learning activity is to research, implement, simulate, analyze, and present an end-to-end framework for Phishing Message Identification and Analysis. Specifically, students developed a dual-engine Python detector, created a standardized dataset of 10 fictional email scenarios (5 phishing, 5 legitimate), executed an independent 7-point audit checklist, compared AI classifications against human verification, and formulated cryptographic and network countermeasures.

2. System Design & Architecture

The software architecture integrates two complementary detection pipelines:

  • Heuristic & Cryptographic Engine: Analyzes email authentication headers (SPF pass/fail, DKIM cryptographic integrity), compares sender vs Reply-To domains, evaluates URL syntax (subdomain deception, raw IP hosts, unencrypted HTTP), and detects macro-enabled attachment threats.
  • Machine Learning & NLP Engine: Employs TF-IDF tokenization (n-grams 1-2) with a trained Multinomial Naive Bayes / Logistic Regression classifier to evaluate keyword weighting and linguistic probability.

5. Security Analysis & Countermeasures

Technical countermeasures must be implemented in a defense-in-depth posture:

  1. Email Cryptographic Frameworks:
    • SPF (RFC 7208): Validates transmitting MTA IP against DNS TXT record.
    • DKIM (RFC 6376): Public-key digital signature verifying header and body integrity.
    • DMARC (RFC 7489): Enforces identifier alignment and dictates rejection (p=reject) of unaligned mail.
  2. Phishing-Resistant MFA: Deployment of FIDO2 / WebAuthn hardware security tokens that enforce cryptographic origin binding to stop credential theft on fake proxy portals.
  3. Operational Protocols: Mandatory Out-of-Band (OOB) dual-channel phone verification for executive wire requests or payroll updates.
