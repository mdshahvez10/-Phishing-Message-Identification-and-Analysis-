2. Email Cryptographic Authentication Principles

Speaker: Student 1 (1:30 - 2:30)

In Cryptography and Network Security, email spoofing is mitigated through three core cryptographic and DNS standards:

ProtocolCryptographic & Network MechanismVulnerability Countered
SPF (RFC 7208) DNS TXT lookup validating sending server IP against authorized range. MTA unauthorized sender forgery.
DKIM (RFC 6376) Asymmetric digital signatures (RSA/Ed25519) computing cryptographic hashes of headers and body. Message tampering and in-transit payload alteration.
DMARC (RFC 7489) Enforces Identifier Alignment between RFC 5322 From and authenticated SPF/DKIM domains (p=reject). Display name spoofing and brand impersonation.
Limitation Highlight: Cryptographic signatures verify *identity*, but attackers can send authentic emails from newly created malicious domains or free webmail (BEC).

6. Countermeasures & Defense-in-Depth

Speaker: Student 3 (5:45 - 6:30)

Cryptographic & Gateway Defenses

  • DMARC Enforcement (p=reject): Stops domain impersonation by requiring strict identifier alignment.
  • FIDO2 / WebAuthn Hardware Keys: Origin-bound public-key authentication makes credential harvesting ineffective.
  • Perimeter Isolation: Automatic sandboxing and macro execution prohibition from untrusted zones.

Safe Responses & Behavioral Rules

  • Mandatory Out-of-Band (OOB) Verification: Never transfer funds or modify payroll based on an email; call known office phone.
  • Inspect Apex Domain: Disregard deceptive prefixes; look directly at domain.tld.
  • Incident Reporting: Forward suspect messages to SOC (abuse@) for automated domain blackholing.
